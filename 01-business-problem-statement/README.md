# 📄 Business Problem Statement

## 📜 A Brief History of Writing Things Down (Before Building Them)
Long before the internet, ancient builders realized that starting a monument without counting the stones usually led to a very expensive pile of rubble. Somewhere around the industrial revolution, this survival instinct evolved into the "Business Case"- a formal way of asking, *"Are we throwing good money after a bad process?"* 

In the tech boom, companies started skipping this step entirely, adopting a philosophy of *"build it fast, fix it later."* But as it turns out, building software without diagnosing the human friction behind it is just a very fast way to alienate 30% of your user base. 

That is exactly why this document was written on June 5, 2026. Before a single user story was prioritised, we needed to stop guessing and state the cold, hard facts: our onboarding funnel was leaking, and it wasn't a technical bug—it was an experience flaw. This artifact was created to anchor the entire sprint team to a commercial reality before anyone touched a line of code.

---

## 🔍 What's Inside This Artifact
This document strips away the corporate noise to outline the absolute ground truth of our product strategy:

*   **The Diagnostic (5 Whys):** A ruthless teardown that bypasses technical excuses to prove that our 32% drop-off rate was caused by a forgotten 2021 web design lazily copy-pasted onto mobile devices.
*   **The Damage Report:** The raw financial cost of the friction—specifically, €2.1M in frozen premium product revenue and a €313K mountain of completely avoidable customer support tickets.
*   **The North Star:** Legally vetted target benchmarks—like slashing drop-offs below 10% and compressing a 4.2-day compliance wait into a tight, 24-hour turnaround window.
